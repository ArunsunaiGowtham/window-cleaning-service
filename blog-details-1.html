How Often Should You Wash Residential Windows?

  • Hana Sato (Quality Inspector)
  • 12 October 2026
  • 5 min read
  1. Home
  2. Blog
  3. How Often Should You Wash Residential Windows?

Most homeowners wait until their glass is noticeably foggy or spotted before calling a professional. However, glass is not an inert, impermeable material—it is a porous silica structure that permanently bonds with minerals over time.

1. The Difference Between Weather Soil and Mineral Etching

Windblown dust and raindrops contain micro-particles of calcium, silica, and airborne pollutants. When left in direct sunlight, the heat of the sun bakes these minerals into the microscopic pores of the glass. Over 6 to 12 months, this process causes stage-two corrosion known as "glass etching"—leaving permanent milky stains that standard washing cannot reverse without abrasive polishing compounds.

3. Why Rain Does Not Actually Make Clean Windows Dirty

A widespread homeowner myth is that rain dirties freshly cleaned glass. In reality, clean rainwater consists of pure condensation that evaporates cleanly. The reason rain appears to dirty windows is that it lands on preexisting dirt, sticky dish soap residues, and pollen films, dissolving the grime and drying into visible droplets. When glass is washed with 0-PPM deionised pure water, rainwater sheets off smoothly without forming spots.

4. Key Indicators That Your Windows Need Immediate Attention

  • White crusting on window sills from irrigation sprinkler overspray.
  • Yellow or greenish dusting on screens during spring and early summer.
  • Sticky interior sliding glass patio doors caused by pet paws and cooking oils.
  • Noticeable reduction in room brightness and interior natural sunlight.
